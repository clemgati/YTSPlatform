# A portrait shoot, start to finish

One job through the whole application: an enquiry arrives, a portrait session is shot, and
the invoice for it is paid. Every step below is something the application can actually do
today, in the order a studio would do it.

Written against the screens as they are. Where the application will not let you do something
yet, it says so rather than pretending.

---

## Before the first enquiry

### 1. Start a studio

On the sign-in screen, **Start a new studio**. An email, a password, and the studio's name.

That name is the account's, and it is the one thing here you cannot change afterwards — the
name on your documents is set separately, in the next step, and is the one clients see.

### 2. Fill in Settings → Your studio

Everything on an invoice comes from here, and an invoice raised before it is filled in
carries the gaps.

| Field | Why it is asked for |
| --- | --- |
| **Studio name** | The name on every document. Separate from the account's name above |
| **You charge in** | The currency for every figure in the application. Changing it later does not convert anything already entered |
| **Email**, **Phone**, **Website**, **Address** | How a client reaches you, printed on the document |
| **Tax registration number** | Required on an invoice in most places if you are registered |
| **How to pay you** | Bank details, or whatever the client needs. There is no payment processing here — this is the text that tells somebody where to send the money |
| **Footer** | Terms, late-payment wording, anything you want at the foot of every document |

### 3. Set the pricing floor — Ledger → Pricing floor

Three numbers:

- **Take-home target** — what you intend to pay yourself for the year, after tax
- **Sellable days a year** — days you can actually *sell*, not days you work. Editing,
  admin and marketing consume days you cannot bill for, so this is usually far lower than
  people expect
- **Effective tax rate** — including self-employment or national insurance. Blank if you
  would rather handle tax separately

From those and your recorded costs it works out the least a day can be sold for without
losing money. Every package is measured against it.

### 4. Check your packages — Ledger → Your packages

Four are there already: wedding, video, real estate, headshots. **None has a price**, on
purpose — a price this application invented would be in a currency you had not chosen and
would then be measured against your own floor as though it meant something.

For a portrait business, **Edit** the headshot package, or **Add a package**:

- **What it is called** — "Portrait session — 60 minutes"
- **Kind of work** — Portrait
- **Shooting time** — what the floor multiplies. Post-production is added on top
- **How many sessions** — 1
- **What you charge** — leave blank if undecided; the floor is shown either way

If the row says **BELOW COST**, the package sells for less than it costs to deliver. That is
the whole reason the floor exists.

### 5. Record your overheads — Ledger → Costs

Insurance, software, rent, accountancy. **Record a cost**, and leave the booking as
"Overhead — not a specific job".

Do this before trusting the floor: with no overheads recorded, the floor is the floor of a
business with no costs, which is not a business.

## The enquiry

### 6. Log it — Dashboard → Log an enquiry

Name, where it came from, what they are asking about, and a budget if they mentioned one.

It appears under **Awaiting your reply** with a clock running. Response time is the strongest
predictor of whether an enquiry books, so that list exists to be emptied rather than admired.

### 7. Reply, then mark it

Reply however you normally would — the application does not send email. Then press
**Replied** on the row.

That stamps the first response and takes it off the urgent list. It stays under **Every
enquiry**, where it can be corrected or removed later.

---

## Turning it into a booking

### 8. Create the client — Clients → Add a client

Account name — the couple, the person, or the company — and the person you actually deal
with. An account can hold several people; the form shows the primary one.

### 9. Open a booking — the client's page → Open a Booking

Name it for the job, not the client: "Okafor — Headshots". Set the kind of work, the status,
and the agreed value if there is one.

**A booking is what everything else hangs off.** No quote, contract, invoice, shoot day or
cost can exist without one, so this step cannot be skipped — if the actions on the Ledger are
greyed out, this is why.

### 10. Send a quote — Ledger → Send a quote

Pick the booking, add a line per thing you are charging for, and set how long the price
holds.

It appears under **Out with clients** with the number and how long it has been waiting. A
quote past its date is flagged, because a price that lapsed while nobody looked is a
conversation you have to reopen.

**If the client asks for a change**, press **Revise** — a quote can be corrected right up
until it is answered. After that it is the record of a decision and cannot be.

### 11. They say yes — press Accepted

This does two things: it marks the quote accepted, and it **raises an invoice from the
quote's lines as a draft**.

A draft, not a demand. Nothing is owed until you send it, so accepting can never put an
unreviewed figure into money owed.

---

## Optionally: hold the date

For portraits this is often skipped. For anything where the date matters:

### 12. Draw up a contract — Ledger → Draw up a contract

Title, retainer, turnaround, revision rounds, cancellation and reschedule terms. A usage
licence if the client is a business licensing the images.

Then **Send**, and **Signed** when it comes back. A contract can be **Corrected** until it is
signed — after that the words are what somebody agreed to.

A signed contract with an unpaid retainer still shows under **Dates not yet held**: a
signature alone does not hold a date. Raise a **Retainer** invoice and record the payment,
and it clears.

## The shoot

### 13. Schedule the shoot day — Sessions → Schedule a session

Pick the booking, set the date, time and place. Add the address or coordinates and the
screen works out the light for that spot on that day, which is worth having on a recce.

### 14. Before the day — the session's page

- **Shot list** — what you promised, grouped so a group can be worked through and released
- **Crew** — anybody else working it, with their call time
- **Kit for the day** — what is going out, ticked off as packed and again as returned
- **Call sheet** — copy it or save it to send

### 15. On and after the day

- **Releases** — anybody recognisable, marked signed or refused. This is the written
  permission to use somebody's face, and it exists nowhere else
- **Files** — record each copy of the cards as you make it. Three copies, on different
  kinds of media, one away from the studio. Press **Checked** when you have actually opened
  a drive and read from it: that is the only thing separating a backup you *have* from one
  you *believe* you have

### 16. Track the hours — the booking's page → Post-production

Add the culling, editing and admin, with an estimate up front and the actual when done.

The estimate is asked for before the work because an estimate written afterwards is a memory
of how long it felt, and it will agree with the actual every time. Once enough tasks are
finished the pricing floor stops assuming how long post-production takes and starts using
your own measured figure.

### 17. Record what the job cost — Ledger → Costs

A second shooter, prints, a studio hire, parking. **Record a cost** and charge it to the
booking rather than leaving it as overhead — a job cost comes out of one booking's margin,
overhead raises the floor under every job.

**Record a journey** for the driving, with the rate your tax authority allows this year. It
is asked for rather than assumed because it differs by country, year and vehicle, and a
figure this application invented would be wrong in a way nobody would check.

## Getting paid

### 18. Deliver — the booking's page → Delivery

**Promise something** for each deliverable — the gallery, the retouched selects, the print
files — with what was agreed and when it is due.

### 19. Send the invoice — Ledger → Money owed

The draft raised when the quote was accepted is under **Raised but not sent**.

- **Edit** to correct anything — a draft is still being written
- **Send** when you are ready. That is the step that puts the figure into money owed and
  starts the clock on the due date
- **Save** writes the document to a file to attach to an email. The application does not
  send it

Once sent it cannot be edited. If the figure is wrong, **Void** it and raise another — an
invoice somebody holds a copy of must not quietly change under the same number.

### 20. Record the payment

When the money arrives, **Record payment** on the invoice: amount, date, method, and a
reference if you have one.

The invoice settles itself — its state is worked out from its payments rather than stored —
and it leaves **Money owed**. Part payments are fine; it stays outstanding for the remainder.

**If you record a payment against the wrong invoice**, it is under **Payments received**,
which lists every payment against every invoice whatever its state. Remove it there and
record it against the right one. That list exists because a misattributed payment settles the
wrong invoice, which drops it off the money-owed list and takes the only route back to the
payment with it.

## What the application will not do yet

Worth knowing before you rely on it:

- **It does not send email.** Quotes and invoices are rendered and saved or shared; the
  sending is yours. Password reset emails are the one exception, and they are limited to
  verified addresses until AWS grants production access
- **It does not take payments.** "How to pay you" is text on a document
- **There is no gallery or proofing.** Deliverables are tracked, not hosted
- **There is one environment.** Every client — web, desktop, Android, iOS — talks to the
  same server and the same database. There is no sandbox to practise in, so the first
  booking you enter is a real one

## If two devices disagree

Everything is kept on the device and copied to the others, so it keeps working with no
connection and catches up afterwards.

If the same record is edited in two places at once, the newer edit wins and the older one is
**set aside rather than discarded** — the Dashboard says so, and Settings → shows what was
put aside so anything that still matters can be typed back in.
